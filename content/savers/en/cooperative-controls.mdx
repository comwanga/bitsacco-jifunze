## Institutions need more than trustworthy individuals

A cooperative may have many members, paid staff, vendors and legal reporting duties. Good intentions cannot replace a system that prevents, detects and corrects errors. Assign authority, keep evidence and separate duties. Board oversight should include conflicts, unusual transactions, service failures, complaints and whether staff have the resources to follow approved procedures.

Membership control does not mean each member performs every operational task. Members need understandable reports and real ways to question elected representatives. Directors should explain delegated authority and reserved member decisions. A vote may approve a policy without authorizing a particular employee to transfer assets alone. Related-party lending and procurement need clear disclosure and review under applicable rules.

## Case: a deposit is not just an app notification

Fictional cooperative Upendo starts with 90,000 member-savings liabilities and 20,000 cash. It receives 10,000 of new deposits and pays 8,000 of withdrawals, with no other activity. Ending savings liabilities are **92,000**; cash is **22,000**. The difference is not missing money: other assets fund the remaining liabilities, and must be verified separately.

A duplicate deposit posting can falsely increase one member's claim without increasing cash. A receipt, bank/mobile/provider statement and member sub-ledger should be linked and reconciled. A reviewer should obtain evidence independently rather than accepting only screenshots selected by the cashier. Correct errors with dated adjustments and explanations, not hidden deletions. Limit who can change member identities, withdrawal destinations or historical records.

## Different assurance roles

Routine controls include authorization limits, reconciliation, access review, backup checks and exception monitoring. Internal audit evaluates whether controls and governance work; it needs sufficient independence from operations. An independent financial audit examines the statements within its scope. It is not a guarantee that every fraud or future failure is impossible. Check actual qualifications, scope, findings and follow-up; an “audited” badge is not enough.

Members need reports covering assets, liabilities, loan quality, expenses, liquidity and material uncertainties. Protect personal loan information while showing aggregate effects and conflicts. A blockchain proof of assets does not demonstrate complete liabilities, lawful control or fair financial presentation. Auditing must reconcile the whole institution, including off-chain contracts.

## Digital services move tasks, not responsibility

A mobile interface, hosted ledger or Bitcoin provider can improve access but introduces permissions, outages, data protection and vendor dependence. The institution should evaluate service terms, incident reporting, data exports, access revocation and continuity if the vendor fails. Use least necessary staff permissions and promptly remove departing staff access. A second screen is not a separate control if it uses the same compromised credentials.

## Try it

Trace a fictional 1,000 deposit: receipt, independently observed asset increase, member-liability entry, reviewer and member statement. Then inject a duplicate ledger entry without a second receipt. **Worked check:** liabilities would be overstated by 1,000 while assets would not increase. Write who investigates, corrects, approves the adjustment and explains it to the affected member. Include an accessible complaints route for someone who cannot use the app.
