## A member balance creates an obligation

A cooperative's assets can include cash, deposits elsewhere, loans receivable and equipment. Liabilities are amounts owed to members or others. Equity is the residual difference after liabilities; accounting and regulatory capital definitions require qualified local interpretation. An ownership share and withdrawable savings can receive different treatment. This lesson uses a deliberately simplified statement, not a filing template.

A loan is an asset because repayment is expected, not because repayment is guaranteed. Borrower income failure, weak underwriting, concentration in one employer or sector, and related-party favoritism can cause losses. Collateral can reduce some losses but may be hard to value, seize or sell. Expected interest is not available cash before it is received.

## Case: enough assets, too little timely cash

Fictional Mwangaza has the following local-unit statement:

| Assets | Amount | Funding | Amount |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| Immediately available cash | 20,000 | Member savings liabilities | 90,000 |
| Loans expected to be repaid | 75,000 | Simplified equity | 10,000 |
| Equipment | 5,000 | | |
| Total | 100,000 | Total | 100,000 |

Members request 30,000 this week. Only 5,000 of loan repayments is expected before the deadline. Even if those repayments arrive, available cash is 25,000: a **5,000 liquidity gap**. Selling equipment may be slow and damage operations; demanding all loans back immediately may be impossible under contracts. The statement's 10,000 equity does not pay a 30,000 withdrawal request by itself.

In a **separate** scenario starting from the original statement, loan losses reduce the 75,000 loan asset by 15,000. Assets become 85,000 against 90,000 liabilities; simplified equity is **−5,000**. This is a solvency problem in the example, not merely a delay. Real provisioning, valuation, regulatory reporting and resolution rules must be checked by qualified professionals.

## Match timing and currency

Borrowing short and lending long can leave an institution unable to meet withdrawals before loans repay: a maturity mismatch. Liquidity planning considers expected and stressed inflows, withdrawals, committed facilities and access to genuinely available funds. A credit line is not reliable just because a spreadsheet assumes it; verify its terms and availability. Credit losses and liquidity stress can reinforce each other.

If liabilities are owed in local currency but assets are held in Bitcoin, a price drop can reduce coverage. A Bitcoin loan instead repaid in sats exposes a borrower with local-currency income to a different repayment burden when the exchange price rises. Changing the asset or loan denomination does not remove borrower default, collection costs, legal duties or the institution's obligations.

## Try it

Recalculate the liquidity case with expected repayments delayed until next month: timely cash is **20,000**, and the gap is **10,000**. Then calculate the separate loan-loss case from the original statement. Explain why a functioning wallet cannot cure either a missing cash inflow or a negative balance-sheet residual. State which information an actual institution would need before deciding what to do.
