A message arrives in Kamau's chama WhatsApp group: "Bitsacco admin here. Send 5,000 shillings of bitcoin to this address and receive 10,000 back within 24 hours. Offer ends tonight!" The profile photo has the Bitsacco logo. It is a <Term id="scam" />.

## Common scams

- **"Double your money" schemes.** Guaranteed daily or weekly returns, often with a fancy dashboard showing your "profits". You can never withdraw.
- **Fake support.** Someone pretending to be from a wallet, an exchange or Bitsacco asks for your seed phrase, PIN or a "verification payment".
- **Impersonation.** A stranger copies the name and photo of a group admin, a pastor or a well-known person.
- **Fake apps and links.** <Term id="phishing" /> messages lead to a website or app that looks real and asks you to type your <Term id="seed-phrase" />.
- **Recruitment bonuses.** You earn by bringing in new members. When new money stops, the scheme collapses.
- **"Mentors" and romance.** A friendly stranger builds trust for weeks, then asks you to invest through their platform.

## Red flags

1. A **guaranteed** return, or a return that sounds too good to be true.
2. **Urgency**: "today only", "last chance".
3. Anyone asking for your **seed phrase**, private key or PIN.
4. You must **send first** to receive more.
5. **Secrecy**: "don't tell your family".
6. Pressure to **recruit** others.

<Callout type="scam" title="The one rule that stops most scams">
Never share your seed phrase, and never send bitcoin to someone who promises to send more back. Bitsacco will never message you first to ask for money or secrets.
</Callout>

## If you are targeted

- Stop. Don't reply, don't click, don't send.
- Check through official channels you find yourself, such as bitsacco.com, never through a link in the message.
- Warn your chama and report the account to the group admin and the platform.

## If you have been scammed

Bitcoin payments can't be reversed. If your seed phrase was exposed, move any remaining funds to a **new** wallet with a **new** seed phrase straight away. Report it to the police and to the service the scammer used. Ignore anyone who offers to "recover" your bitcoin for a fee; that is the second half of the same scam.

## Try it

At your next chama meeting, one person reads Kamau's message out loud. Everyone else names as many red flags as they can. Then agree on one sentence your group will use to warn each other.
