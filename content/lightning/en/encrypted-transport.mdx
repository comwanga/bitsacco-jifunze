When Alice connected to Bob in lesson 3, you typed `BOB_PUBKEY@bob:9735`. That <Term id="public-key" /> wasn't just an address: it is how Alice makes sure she is talking to the real Bob, over a link nobody else can read.

## Why encrypt?

Lightning messages reveal a lot: which channels you have, which payments you are forwarding, which HTLCs are pending. Without encryption, anyone on the network path could watch, or even change, them. BOLT 8 gives every peer connection **confidentiality** (nobody can read it), **integrity** (nobody can change it unnoticed) and **authentication** (you know who is on the other end).

## The Noise handshake, in three acts

Lightning uses the **Noise_XK** pattern. "K" means Alice already knows Bob's static public key.

1. **Act one.** Alice sends a fresh, one-time public key, and proves she knows Bob's static key.
2. **Act two.** Bob replies with his own one-time key. Both now share a secret that nobody watching can compute.
3. **Act three.** Alice sends her static key, encrypted, and proves she owns it.

After 50 + 50 + 66 bytes, both sides have encryption keys, which are rotated every 1,000 messages. Only then does the `init` message from last lesson flow.
