Mama Wanjiru sells vegetables at the market. Over the years she has kept her savings in a clay tin under the bed, then in M-Pesa, then in a SACCO. Each time she trusted someone: the tin to stay hidden, Safaricom to keep its records, the SACCO to stay honest. That trust is the story of money.

## The three jobs of money

Anything that works as money does three jobs:

1. **Medium of exchange.** You can swap it for goods and services. The seller of tomatoes accepts it.
2. **Store of value.** You can keep it and still buy something with it later.
3. **Unit of account.** Prices are measured in it: "a bunch of sukuma is 20 shillings".

Cattle, salt and shells have all been money. They worked until they were too hard to carry, divide or keep.

## Who controls the shilling

The Central Bank of Kenya issues banknotes and coins and conducts monetary policy. Money also includes commercial-bank deposits: bank lending can create a deposit, subject to financial and regulatory constraints. A mobile-money balance is another kind of claim on a provider, with its own rules; it is not the same as cash in your hand. Similar distinctions matter in other countries.

These institutions support payments, saving and credit, but access depends on their systems and rules. A sustained rise in the general price level is <Term id="inflation" />: the same amount buys less. Its causes include demand, supply disruptions and monetary conditions; it is not simply proof that one institution printed too much.

## What Bitcoin changes

<Term id="bitcoin">Bitcoin</Term> is money that no single company or government issues. Its rules are written in open software that anyone can read:

- Under the current consensus rules, issuance is limited to about 21 million bitcoin.
- New issuance follows block height, not exact calendar dates. Blocks arrive at uneven times; no miner can make validating nodes accept a larger subsidy than the rules permit.
- Computers called <Term id="node">nodes</Term> independently validate the shared <Term id="blockchain" />. Some retain its entire history; others discard older block data after checking it.

In 2008 someone using the name Satoshi Nakamoto described this in a nine-page paper. The goal was money that two people can send to each other directly, without asking a bank for permission.

<Callout type="tip" title="Bitcoin is not a company">
There is no "Bitcoin office" that can reset a lost spending key. Self-custody reduces dependence on an account operator, but providers can freeze custodial accounts and theft, coercion, network disruption or lost keys can still prevent access. That responsibility is what the next lessons explore.
</Callout>

## What Bitcoin does not promise

Bitcoin is not a plan to get rich. Its price in shillings moves up and down a lot, as the next lesson explains. Jifunze teaches how it works so that you can make your own decisions; it never tells you what to buy.
