Lightning is private by design: balances aren't gossiped, and onions hide senders and receivers. But privacy here is a matter of degree, and a curious or hostile node has tricks.

## Probing

Alice can send Dina a payment with a **random** payment hash. It can never settle, because nobody knows the preimage. Look at how it fails:

- If it reaches Dina, she says "I don't know this payment" (`incorrect_payment_details`).
- If a channel on the way is too small, a node says so earlier.

So each failure answers a yes-or-no question: can this amount get through? Ask enough questions and you learn a channel's balance. That is <Term id="probing" />.

<Exercise id="ch16-probe" />

Your binary search should land within 20,000 sats of the route's real limit, without ever paying a sat.

## Other attacks

- **Channel jamming.** An attacker sends many payments to themselves and never settles them, filling channels' HTLC slots (483 per side) or locking up <Term id="liquidity" /> until the time locks expire. Proposed defences include upfront fees and reputation for peers.
- **Fee and timelock manipulation.** An attacker can try to make an <Term id="htlc" /> expire before it can be claimed on-chain when fees are high. Anchor outputs let nodes raise fees on their closing transactions.
- **Privacy leaks.** Your node's public channels, aliases and on-chain funding outputs can be linked to you. Private channels, onion services (Tor) and careful coin selection help.
- **Eclipse attacks.** A node fed only by attacker-controlled peers can be shown a false view of the chain. Connect to several honest Bitcoin nodes.
