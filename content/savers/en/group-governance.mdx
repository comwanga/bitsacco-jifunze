## Agree while there is no dispute

A group constitution records purpose, eligibility, contributions, payout or lending rules, meeting notice, quorum, votes, complaints and exit. Quorum is the minimum participation needed for a valid meeting; a voting threshold determines whether a proposal passes. They answer different questions. State whether a majority means votes cast, members present or all members, and how abstentions and conflicts count.

Membership rules need due dates, treatment of late payments and hardship, correction of errors, joining mid-cycle, death, incapacity and departure. Penalties should be explicit and reviewable, not invented by an official during an argument. Changes to contribution obligations, a treasury asset or key control may need a different process from buying stationery. Local legal requirements must also be checked where relevant.

## Four jobs, more than one person

The person requesting a payment should provide its purpose and evidence. A separate authorized decision approves it. The payer executes the approved payment; a record keeper posts it; another member checks the records against independently obtained statements or wallet information. Small groups may combine some roles, but should identify the resulting weakness and add a practical review rather than pretend independence exists.

A second signature on a transaction does not prove the member vote happened. Keep meeting minutes, payment approvals and financial records linked by a reference number. Restrict access to personal information. All members can receive understandable financial summaries without every private circumstance becoming public.

## Case: a vote and a missing contribution

Fictional Umoja has 12 members. Its written rule says quorum is eight and ordinary proposals pass by a majority of votes cast. Nine attend: seven vote yes and two no. Quorum is met and the motion passes. If changing the treasury policy instead needs two-thirds of **all** members, it needs eight yes votes: seven is insufficient. These are invented bylaws, not universal cooperative or national law.

The same meeting's ledger lists ten contributions of 500: **5,000 expected**. Independent receipts show only nine: **4,500 received**. Record the 500 discrepancy for investigation; do not invent a tenth receipt or silently charge a member. Check dates, duplicates, the bank/mobile statement, unconfirmed transactions and posting mistakes. A discrepancy is evidence to investigate, not proof a named person stole money.

## Make disagreement survivable

Define who receives a complaint, how evidence is preserved, when a response is due, who must recuse themselves and how an appeal is heard. Avoid publishing accusations or sensitive debts in a public chat. A confidential hardship process can protect dignity while keeping aggregate financial effects visible. If the usual reviewer is involved, use the agreed independent alternative.

Bitcoin does not replace these processes. It can make some transaction evidence independently visible, but it does not explain a payment's purpose or prove a fair decision. A blockchain balance does not list every promise to members.

## Try it

Write a fictional payment record with request, member decision, authorized payer, transaction reference, ledger entry and reviewer. Apply both Umoja voting rules. **Worked check:** the ordinary motion passes; the two-thirds-of-all-members motion fails. Reconcile 5,000 expected with 4,500 received and list two possible explanations before drawing conclusions.
