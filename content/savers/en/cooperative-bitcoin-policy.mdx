## Ask what is actually proposed

“Integrating Bitcoin” can describe very different activities. An institution holding an asset for its own treasury is different from custody for members, arranging a third-party service, processing payments, or lending. Each changes counterparties, obligations, permissions and operational work. A successful wallet demo establishes technical feasibility only, not institutional authorization.

No SACCO or credit union should infer legal permission from this lesson. Establish country, charter, governing law, regulator, activity and customer/member scope. Ask qualified local advisers about permitted investments, deposit-taking, custody, lending, capital/liquidity rules, consumer protection, privacy, tax, accounting, reporting, sanctions and financial-crime obligations **where applicable**. Record actual authorities and dates. Member votes do not override legal restrictions.

The NCUA reference in further reading concerns the United States and distinguishes institutional authority, third-party activity and insurance coverage. It does not authorize an East African cooperative or every US charter to provide the same service. Rules can change; a generic educational page cannot supply current approval.

## Four separate proposals

| Proposal | Principal questions |
| --- | --- |
| Treasury asset | Which funds may bear losses, who authorizes, what liabilities must be covered, and is holding it permitted? |
| Custody for members | Who owns assets, how are customer records and assets distinguished, what recovery/complaints duties apply, and is custody permitted? |
| Payments or a third-party arrangement | Who converts and settles, what happens on delay/refund, what fees and disclosures apply, and which party is responsible? |
| Lending or collateral | Who owes which currency, how are borrowers assessed, can collateral be controlled and liquidated lawfully, and is the activity permitted? |

Do not label member Bitcoin as an insured deposit without establishing the actual scheme and coverage. Do not use withdrawable member savings as risk-bearing treasury capital simply because they sit in the same app. Product names do not determine legal or accounting treatment.

## Case: collateral does not erase credit risk

In a fictional analytical case, a local-currency loan is 100,000 and Bitcoin collateral is initially quoted at 150,000. A 50% collateral-price drop leaves **75,000**, a **25,000 uncovered principal gap** before liquidation costs or interest. A margin call cannot guarantee that a distressed borrower supplies more collateral, that markets remain liquid, or that the lender has lawful and timely access. This case grants no permission to make such a loan.

If instead the loan is owed in sats, a borrower paid in local currency can face a rising local-currency repayment cost. Repeated refinancing or optimistic price assumptions can hide unaffordable debt. Bitcoin does not assess income, enforce a repayment contract, prevent default or provide emergency liquidity by itself.

## A decision memo, including a no-go outcome

Document purpose, alternatives including existing payment rails, authority evidence, affected member rights, asset/liability denomination, stressed liquidity, custody, vendor failures, costs and exit. Qualified accountants must determine the actual recognition, valuation, impairment, income, disclosure and tax rules. Track sats and reporting-currency values with price sources, times and fees; a ledger demo is not compliant financial reporting.

## Try it

Choose **one** proposal from the table and write a paper-only decision memo. List unanswered authority questions before any technical steps. Calculate the collateral stress case and an outage preventing sale. **Worked check:** 150,000 × 0.5 = 75,000; 100,000 − 75,000 = 25,000 uncovered before costs. A responsible conclusion can be “insufficient authority or protection; do not proceed.”
