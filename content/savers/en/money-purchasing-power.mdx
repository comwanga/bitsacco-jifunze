## The same unit can represent different claims

A banknote, a bank deposit and a mobile-money balance may all be quoted in local currency. They have different issuers, access rules and failure risks. Commercial banks can create deposit money when lending; capital, liquidity, profitability, borrower demand and regulation constrain them. A loan from an ordinary savings group usually transfers an existing fund rather than automatically creating bank deposits. Do not apply a bank money-creation explanation to every lender.

Bitcoin is a different monetary asset and settlement network. Self-custodied sats are not a bank's promise to pay local currency. A custodial app's Bitcoin balance is a claim on its operator. A stablecoin is also not Bitcoin, and its currency peg and redemption depend on its particular design and issuer. Similar-looking balances can carry very different obligations.

## Nominal value and purchasing power

Nominal value is the number of currency units. Purchasing power asks what those units buy. An inflation index follows a defined basket, not every family's exact spending. A one-off rise in tomatoes alone is not the same as sustained general inflation. Demand, production costs, disruptions, expectations and monetary conditions can interact.

In this fictional one-year example, a basket costs 1,000 at the start and 1,100 at the end: inflation for that basket is (1,100 ÷ 1,000 − 1) × 100 = **10%**. An unchanged 1,000 balance now buys 1,000 ÷ 1,100 = **0.909 baskets**. That is about 9.09% less basket-buying power, not exactly 10% less.

If savings grow from 1,000 to 1,050, their nominal gain is 5%. Their real change is (1.05 ÷ 1.10 − 1) × 100 = **−4.55%**, before taxes and fees. Subtracting 10 from 5 is only an approximation. There is no claim that these rates will occur.

## Depreciation is a different comparison

An exchange rate compares currencies. If one fictional foreign unit changes from costing 100 local units to 120, local currency has depreciated against it. Imported inputs can become costlier, but local prices need not change by the same percentage. Wages, local production, contracts and other prices also matter. A Bitcoin/local-currency quote combines Bitcoin market movement with the local currency's exchange relationships.

Central-bank policy can influence borrowing costs, demand and monetary conditions. Higher interest rates can restrain demand while increasing some borrowers' costs; supply shortages cannot always be solved by a rate change. Monetary systems also provide payment infrastructure and credit. Evaluating their performance requires evidence about a place and period, not a claim that all inflation has one cause or Bitcoin fixes it.

## Compare the complete saving arrangement

Ask what pays the future expense, when access is available, whether principal or returns are promised and by whom, and what fees, taxes, default and price risks apply. Bitcoin's limited issuance does not promise that the price of a food basket in sats will fall. Demand may fall and purchasing power may remain below your starting point for years.

## Try it

A second fictional basket rises from 2,000 to 2,160 while savings rise from 2,000 to 2,100. Calculate both changes. **Worked check:** basket inflation is 8%, nominal savings growth is 5%, and real change is 1.05 ÷ 1.08 − 1 = **−2.78%** approximately. Describe one reason your own expenses might behave differently.
