The book draws Lightning as a stack. Each layer relies only on the one below it, like the floors of a building. Read it from the bottom up:

| Layer | What it does | Lessons | BOLTs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Network connection** | Encrypted, authenticated links between nodes | 14 | 8 |
| **Messaging** | The format of every message on the wire | 13 | 1 |
| **Peer-to-peer** | Channel announcements and updates, the <Term id="gossip" /> | 11 | 7 |
| **Routing** | Finding a path and sending it in layers, by <Term id="onion-routing" /> | 10, 12 | 4 |
| **Payment channels** | Funding, commitments, HTLCs, closing | 7, 8, 9 | 2, 3, 5 |
| **Payments** | What users see: an <Term id="invoice" />, an amount, a receipt | 15 | 11 |

Underneath it all sits **Bitcoin**, which enforces the channels.

## Why layers

Layers let developers improve one part without rewriting the rest. A new invoice format (like BOLT 12 offers) doesn't change how channels are funded. A faster pathfinding algorithm doesn't change the wire format.

They also make debugging possible. When a payment fails, you ask: was it the connection, gossip (the sender didn't know a channel), routing (no path with enough liquidity), or the channel itself?
