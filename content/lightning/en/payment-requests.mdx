You have pasted many invoices by now. A <Term id="bolt11" /> <Term id="invoice" /> is a bech32 string with three parts:

```text
lnbcrt500u 1 pj9x…(data)…  (signature)
└── hrp ─┘ ↑ └──── tagged fields ────┘
       separator
```

## The human-readable part (hrp)

- `ln`: Lightning.
- `bc` mainnet, `tb` testnet, `tbs` signet, `bcrt` regtest.
- An optional **amount** with a **multiplier**: `m` milli (10⁻³ BTC), `u` micro (10⁻⁶), `n` nano (10⁻⁹), `p` pico (10⁻¹²).

So `lnbcrt500u` means 500 × 10⁻⁶ BTC = 0.0005 BTC = 50,000 sats. The separator is the **last** `1` in the string; bech32 data never contains a `1`.

## Tagged fields

After a timestamp, the data holds tagged fields: `p` the payment hash (the hash of the <Term id="preimage" />), `d` a description, `x` expiry, `c` the final time lock, `s` a payment secret, `r` routing hints for private channels, and `9` feature bits. The invoice ends with Dina's signature, which also reveals her public key.

## Beyond BOLT11: offers

BOLT 11 invoices are single-use and carry everything in one string. **BOLT 12 offers** are reusable: a shop prints one offer, and each payer's wallet fetches a fresh invoice over Lightning itself. That allows refunds, recurring payments and more privacy for the receiver.
