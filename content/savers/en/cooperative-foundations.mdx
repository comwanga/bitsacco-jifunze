## The institution belongs to members

A cooperative is organized to meet members' shared needs through joint ownership and democratic control. In cooperative finance, members may save, borrow and elect accountable representatives. SACCO commonly expands to savings and credit cooperative organization; credit unions are another form of member-oriented cooperative finance. Their permitted activities, membership, supervision and protection depend on the applicable jurisdiction and charter. Similar principles do not make every institution legally identical.

The International Cooperative Alliance describes principles including voluntary membership, democratic member control, economic participation, autonomy, education, cooperation and concern for community. It identifies one-member-one-vote for primary cooperatives. A real institution's lawful voting procedure, eligibility and delegated responsibilities must still be checked in its statutes and local framework. A Bitcoin signing key does not replace those rights.

## Three different member relationships

An ownership share represents a stake under the institution's rules. Withdrawable savings can be a liability the institution owes the member. A loan is an amount the member owes the institution. These are not interchangeable balances. A share may have transfer, redemption or loss-absorption rules different from a savings account. Do not promise immediate repayment simply because an app labels both amounts “member money.”

Management operates services, directors govern within their mandate, and members exercise rights through the required procedures. Internal controls and independent audit help members assess whether records and decisions are trustworthy. Member ownership does not make mistakes, conflicts, fraud or insolvency impossible. Ask how board conflicts are disclosed, accounts are examined and complaints can be escalated.

## Compare arrangements by structure

| Arrangement | What to investigate |
| --- | --- |
| Small rotating or accumulating savings group | Personal contribution rules, payout cycle and shared records; not automatically a licensed institution |
| SACCO or credit union | Member ownership, governing statute, savings/loan contracts, supervision and applicable member protections |
| Commercial bank | Corporate ownership, depositor contracts, prudential rules and applicable protection; depositors are not automatically its owners |
| Bitcoin-native community | Actual legal organization, rights and liabilities; common technology alone creates none of these |

These structures can serve overlapping needs without being the same. A cooperative may use banks or mobile-money providers for payment access, and a bank customer may also be a cooperative member. Bitcoin is a monetary network, not by itself a lender, elected board, insurer or dispute-resolution institution.

## Case: a member's two balances

Fictional member Njeri's cooperative statement shows 2,000 in ownership shares, 8,000 in withdrawable savings and a loan balance of 5,000. It is misleading to say “Njeri can withdraw 10,000 now” without checking share redemption, savings conditions, any lawful loan set-off and available liquidity. It is equally wrong to erase her membership rights because she is a borrower. The institution needs clear, separately explained contracts and a fair complaints process.

## Try it

Draw three boxes for Njeri: ownership, money owed to her and money she owes. **Worked check:** the figures are 2,000, 8,000 and 5,000 respectively; an immediate withdrawal amount cannot be inferred from these figures alone. Write the documents and questions needed to establish rights. Use this exercise to compare an institution near you without assuming that its name supplies the answers.
