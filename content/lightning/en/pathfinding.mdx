Last lesson Alice read the map. Now she must pick a route. The sender picks the whole route in Lightning, not the network, and it has to work without knowing anyone's balances.

## A shortest-path problem, with a twist

Think of each channel direction as a road with a toll, the routing <Term id="fee" />. Finding the cheapest route is a classic shortest-path problem, and Dijkstra's algorithm solves it: always extend the cheapest path found so far until you reach the destination.

The twist: the map shows capacity, not <Term id="liquidity" />. The cheapest route on paper may fail because a <Term id="channel" /> lacks balance on the right side.

## How real nodes do better

- **Probabilistic cost.** Besides fees, nodes score how likely each channel is to have enough balance. Bigger channels relative to the amount look safer.
- **Mission control.** When an attempt fails, the error says which channel failed. The node remembers and avoids it for a while, then retries. (You'll use this in lesson 16.)
- **Multipart payments (MPP).** A large payment can be split into several smaller parts over different routes, each more likely to fit. Dina only releases the preimage once every part has arrived.
