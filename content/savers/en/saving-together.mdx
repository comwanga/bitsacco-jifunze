Every month, the twelve women of the Tumaini <Term id="chama" /> meet, contribute 1,000 shillings each and hand the pot to one member. The treasurer keeps a book; the secretary writes the minutes. It works because they know each other, and because the rules are clear.

## What a chama already does well

Chamas come in many forms: merry-go-rounds, table banking, investment groups. The best ones share three habits:

- **Clear rules**, agreed before the first shilling.
- **Records** that every member can check.
- **Shared control**, so no single official can disappear with the money.

Bitcoin doesn't replace these habits. It gives them new tools.

## Saving as a group in sats

A group can agree to save in sats instead of, or as well as, shillings. Two big questions come first.

**Who holds the keys?**

| Option | How it works | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One treasurer | One wallet, one seed phrase | One person can lose or take everything |
| <Term id="multisig" /> | For example 2 of 3 officials must sign every withdrawal | Officials must keep their own seed phrases safe |
| A <Term id="federation" /> | Trusted guardians hold the group's bitcoin together | Choose guardians the members trust |

**What do we count in?** Because of <Term id="volatility" />, 1,000 shillings of sats this month may be worth 800 or 1,200 next month. Decide in advance whether each member owes a fixed amount of **shillings** or a fixed amount of **sats**, and what happens when a member leaves after the price has moved.

<Callout type="tip" title="Write it down first">
Agree on your rules and write them in the minutes book before the first contribution. Disagreements are much easier to avoid than to settle.
</Callout>

## Questions your constitution should answer

1. How much does each member contribute, how often, and in shillings or sats?
2. Who holds the keys, and how many signatures does a withdrawal need?
3. How do members see the group's balance and history?
4. What happens if a member wants to leave, or stops contributing?
5. What happens if an official loses their phone or seed phrase?

## Try it

With your chama, or with two friends, draft answers to the five questions above in under 30 minutes. Keep the draft; you'll be surprised how much it helps.
