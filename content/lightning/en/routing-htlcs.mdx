Alice wants to pay Dina 21,000 sats. They have no channel, but Alice → Bob → Chan → Dina is a path. Why would Bob and Chan, strangers to Alice, pass the money on honestly?

## The promise: an HTLC

1. Dina picks a random secret, the <Term id="preimage" />, and puts its hash in her <Term id="invoice" />.
2. Alice offers Bob an <Term id="htlc" />: "21,002 sats are yours if you show me the secret behind this hash before block height *H*."
3. Bob offers Chan the same deal for 21,001 sats, with an earlier deadline. Chan offers Dina 21,000 sats, earlier still.
4. Dina knows the secret, so she reveals it to Chan and claims her 21,000. Chan now knows it and claims from Bob; Bob claims from Alice.

Each hop keeps 1 sat as its fee. Nobody can claim without the secret, and the secret only travels backwards once Dina has been paid. If anything fails, the deadlines pass and every HTLC is cancelled: the whole payment happens, or none of it does.

## Why the deadlines shrink

Bob must have time to claim from Alice *after* Chan claims from him. So each hop's time lock is shorter than the one before it, by that node's `time_lock_delta` (you saw 80 blocks for LND in lesson 5).
