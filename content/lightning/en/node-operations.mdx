Lesson 2 showed a payment paying a small <Term id="fee" /> at every hop. Those fees are set by the <Term id="node">nodes</Term> that forward the payment, and earning them takes some work.

## Routing fees

Every channel direction has a fee policy with two parts:

- a **base fee**, a fixed amount per payment (in millisats), and
- a **fee rate**, in parts per million (ppm) of the amount.

For a 100,000-sat payment through a node charging 1 sat + 100 ppm:

```text
fee = 1 sat + 100,000 × 100 / 1,000,000 = 1 + 10 = 11 sats
```

Set fees too high and senders route around you; too low and you earn nothing for locking up your coins.

`feereport` lists each channel's new policy. The change is announced to the network, so other nodes will use it when they plan routes.

## Backups

A node's channels exist only as signed transactions in its database. If the disk dies, a seed phrase alone gets back the on-chain wallet, but **not** the channels.

A **static channel backup** (SCB) records who your channels were with. After a disaster, your node uses it to ask each peer to close the channel, so you get your funds on-chain. It can't restore the channels themselves, and it is only as good as its last update, so keep it current.

<Callout type="warning" title="Never restore an old database">
Copying an old node database back can broadcast a revoked state, which the other side can punish by taking the whole channel. Use the static channel backup instead.
