When Achieng lost her phone, she panicked: "My bitcoin is gone!" It wasn't. Her bitcoin was never inside the phone. Because she had written down twelve words on paper, she installed the wallet app on a new phone, typed the words in, and everything came back.

## What a wallet really stores

Your bitcoin lives on the <Term id="blockchain" />, the shared record kept by nodes around the world. A <Term id="wallet" /> is an app or device that stores the **keys** that let you spend it.

- A <Term id="private-key" /> is a secret number. It signs your payments, like a signature on a cheque that nobody can forge.
- A <Term id="public-key" /> is made from the private key. From it, the wallet makes an <Term id="address" />, which is what you give people so they can pay you.

| Thing | Like | Share it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Address | Your M-Pesa number | Yes |
| Private key | Your signature and PIN together | Never |
| Seed phrase | The master copy of every key | Never |

## The seed phrase

Most wallets show you 12 or 24 ordinary words when you first set them up. This <Term id="seed-phrase" /> can recreate every key the wallet will ever make. **Whoever knows these words owns the money**, on any phone, anywhere, forever. There is no customer care that can reset it.

<Callout type="scam" title="Nobody real will ever ask for your words">
No wallet company, no Bitsacco staff member and no "support agent" will ever ask for your seed phrase or private key. Anyone who asks is trying to steal from you.
</Callout>

## Making a good backup

A <Term id="backup" /> is only useful if it survives and stays secret:

1. Write the words **on paper**, in order, with their numbers. Check every word twice.
2. Never photograph them, never type them into WhatsApp, email or notes, and never store them in the cloud.
3. Keep the paper somewhere safe from fire, water and curious eyes. Some people keep a second copy in a different trusted place.
4. Before saving much, test it: restore the wallet from the words with a small amount in it.

## Try it

Practise with **pretend** words, not a real wallet. Open any book, pick 12 random words, and write them in order on a card with their numbers. Hide the card. In a week, find it and read the words back. Was it easy to find? Was every word readable? Then tear it up.
