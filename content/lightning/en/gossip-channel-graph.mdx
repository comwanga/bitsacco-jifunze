To find a route to Dina, Alice needs a map: which <Term id="node">nodes</Term> exist and which <Term id="channel">channels</Term> connect them. Nobody publishes that map. Every node builds its own from <Term id="gossip" />, messages it hears from its peers and passes on.

## Three gossip messages

| Message | Announces | Signed by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `channel_announcement` | A channel exists between two nodes, with proof it is funded on-chain | Both nodes and both funding keys |
| `node_announcement` | A node's alias, colour, addresses and features | The node |
| `channel_update` | One direction's fee policy, time lock and whether it is enabled | The node on that side |

A channel is announced only after its funding transaction has six confirmations. That stops nodes from spamming the map with channels that don't exist: anyone can check the funding output on the blockchain.

## Capacity, not balance

The graph shows each channel's **capacity**, the funding amount, but never how it's split between the two sides. Balances change with every payment and would reveal who paid whom. This is why senders sometimes try a route and fail: the map says the channel is big enough, but the balance on the needed side isn't.
