Every way of holding bitcoin answers one question: **who holds the keys?** The answer decides who can spend the money, who can freeze it, and what happens if something goes wrong.

## 1. Someone else holds them: custodial

With a <Term id="custodial" />, a company such as an <Term id="exchange" /> holds the keys for you. You log in with a password, like a bank. It is easy, and if you forget your password they can help.

But the company can freeze your account, be hacked, or close down. Bitcoiners say: "Not your keys, not your coins."

## 2. You hold them: self-custody

With <Term id="self-custody" />, your own wallet holds the keys and you keep the seed phrase. There is no account operator who can freeze that wallet balance. Lost or stolen keys, coercion, network problems and restricted conversion services can still stop you using its value.

There is no central key-reset service. Losing every usable key and backup can make funds inaccessible; someone who steals sufficient spending keys can take them. Losing only a backup does not destroy a still-working wallet, but it is a reason to arrange a safe replacement backup promptly.

## 3. Your community holds them: Fedimint

<Term id="fedimint" /> uses shared custody. Trusted operators called <Term id="guardian">guardians</Term> run a <Term id="federation" />. Its Bitcoin wallet uses <Term id="multisig" />: a configured number of signing keys is required. This spreads control but enough colluding or compromised signers can still take the funds.

Members hold <Term id="ecash" />, digital notes redeemable through the federation. Blind signatures improve payment privacy, but network, gateway and deposit/withdrawal information can still reveal activity. Members depend on the guardians and usable redemption services; software does not enforce every social rule a chama agrees on.

The risk: if most guardians collude, are hacked or go offline together, members can lose access.

| | Custodial | Self-custody | Fedimint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who holds keys | A company | You | Several guardians together |
| Access restrictions | Provider can restrict withdrawals | No account operator; key, network and other access risks remain | Guardian failures, sufficient collusion or service restrictions can block redemption |
| If you lose your phone | Company restores access | Your seed phrase restores it | You need a safely backed-up wallet recovery key or seed and a supported recovery process. The federation's bitcoin alone is insufficient; recovery is not guaranteed. |
| Main risk | The company fails or freezes you | You lose your seed phrase | Most guardians fail together |

<Callout type="warning" title="Ask before you deposit">
Before you use any Bitcoin service, ask: who holds the keys, how many of them, and what happens if they disappear? If nobody can answer clearly, be careful.
</Callout>

## Try it

Make a list of every place you keep money: M-Pesa, a bank, a SACCO, a chama box, any Bitcoin app. For each one, write who holds the keys and what would happen if they shut down tomorrow.
