Mama Wanjiru sells vegetables at the market. Over the years she has kept her savings in a clay tin under the bed, then in M-Pesa, then in a SACCO. Each time she trusted someone: the tin to stay hidden, Safaricom to keep its records, the SACCO to stay honest. That trust is the story of money.

## The three jobs of money

Anything that works as money does three jobs:

1. **Medium of exchange.** You can swap it for goods and services. The seller of tomatoes accepts it.
2. **Store of value.** You can keep it and still buy something with it later.
3. **Unit of account.** Prices are measured in it: "a bunch of sukuma is 20 shillings".

Cattle, salt and shells have all been money. They worked until they were too hard to carry, divide or keep.

## Who controls the shilling

The Central Bank of Kenya issues the shilling and decides how much of it exists. Banks and M-Pesa keep the records of who owns what. This works well most days, but it means a few institutions can freeze an account, reverse a payment or create more money. Over time prices rise, so 1,000 shillings buys less than it did ten years ago. That is <Term id="inflation" />.

## What Bitcoin changes

<Term id="bitcoin">Bitcoin</Term> is money that no single company or government issues. Its rules are written in open software that anyone can read:

- Only about 21 million bitcoin will ever exist.
- New bitcoin is released on a fixed schedule that nobody can speed up.
- Thousands of computers around the world, called <Term id="node">nodes</Term>, each keep a full copy of the record, the <Term id="blockchain" />, and check every payment against the rules.

In 2008 someone using the name Satoshi Nakamoto described this in a nine-page paper. The goal was money that two people can send to each other directly, without asking a bank for permission.

<Callout type="tip" title="Bitcoin is not a company">
There is no "Bitcoin office" to call. Nobody can freeze your bitcoin, but nobody can reset your password either. That freedom comes with responsibility, which the next lessons are about.
</Callout>

## What Bitcoin does not promise

Bitcoin is not a plan to get rich. Its price in shillings moves up and down a lot, as the next lesson explains. Jifunze teaches how it works so that you can make your own decisions; it never tells you what to buy.
