## A balance is not yet a paid bill

A unit of account measures a bill; a payment rail carries value; an asset is what you hold. A school can quote fees in local currency while accepting a payment service that converts Bitcoin. The household still owes the quoted bill unless the contract says otherwise. An invoice being generated, a transaction being broadcast and a recipient having spendable funds are different events.

Self-custodial on-chain payments need usable keys, connectivity and transaction fees. Miners select transactions; paying a fee does not guarantee an exact confirmation time. Fees depend strongly on transaction size and demand for block space, not simply a percentage of the amount sent. Confirm the address, network, amount and final destination on a trusted display. A copied address or a convincing screenshot is not evidence of settlement.

## Lightning adds a different access path

Lightning can settle small payments quickly when a usable route and sufficient directional liquidity exist. A displayed total balance does not guarantee the capacity to receive or send a particular payment. Channels have reserves and other constraints, routing can fail, and opening or closing can require on-chain fees and time. Custodial Lightning hides some operational work but adds provider withdrawal, availability and solvency risk. Wallet implementations have different limits and recovery requirements.

Before retrying a payment after a connection failure, check its status and whether the recipient was paid. A stale screen can lead to duplicate attempts. Avoid sharing invoices or transaction histories publicly when they reveal personal details. Bitcoin transactions are publicly recorded; Lightning is not a blanket promise of anonymity.

## Case: a family payment across borders

Fictional siblings in Nairobi and Madrid compare how to deliver 7,000 local units for a family repair. One has Bitcoin worth 10,000 at the example starting quote. Assume it falls 30%, leaving a quoted value of 7,000. A fictional conversion fee of 2% leaves 6,860; a fixed cash-out cost of 100 leaves **6,760**. The repair is short by **240**. This is a stress test, not today's quote or a prediction. Real spreads, fee bases, rounding, taxes, limits and receiving costs must be checked separately.

Even 10,000 displayed in a bank or mobile app may be inaccessible during an outage. Compare the **net amount available by the deadline**, not just a cheap advertised transfer fee. A foreign exchange provider, cooperative transfer or ordinary bank payment can be more suitable for a particular bill; Bitcoin need not be the chosen route.

## Prepare the failure path

List the deadline, acceptable recipient payment methods, verification steps, likely delays and a contact route that does not rely on the failed device. Do not assume a second app is independent if it uses the same custodian or network. Keep financial records without publishing account identifiers. A reserve plan is useful only if the authorized person can execute it during illness, a lost phone or a service outage.

## Try it

Repeat the case with no price change and the same fictional costs: 10,000 × 0.98 − 100 = **9,700**, leaving 2,700 after the repair. Then make the conversion service unavailable for three days while the bill is due tomorrow. Explain why arithmetic surplus and timely access are different tests.
