You started with one invoice. Since then you have opened and closed channels, followed a payment through HTLCs, built an onion, read the gossip graph, written a route finder, parsed raw messages, decoded invoices and probed a balance, all on real nodes.

## Where Lightning is heading

- **Better payment formats.** BOLT 12 offers, blinded paths that hide the receiver, and async payments for phones that are often offline.
- **Easier liquidity.** Splicing (resizing a channel without closing it), dual-funded channels, and liquidity markets.
- **Communities and custody.** Fedimint and ecash bring Lightning to groups that share trust, like chamas, without each member running a node.

## Build something

The <Term id="lightning" /> is <Term id="open-source" />, and so is everything in this lab. The best way to keep learning is to ship a small tool and get feedback:

1. Pick a brief from the builders path's final project, such as a chama tip jar, a UTXO explainer or an offline sats calculator, or invent your own.
2. Build it against this lab: it's the same regtest network you already know.
3. Share the repository in the Bitsacco community and ask for a review.

<Callout type="tip" title="Keep the lab">
Your lab keeps its channels between sessions. `./lab down` stops it; `./lab down -v` wipes it for a fresh start; `./lab reset --hard route` rebuilds the chain from scratch.
</Callout>
