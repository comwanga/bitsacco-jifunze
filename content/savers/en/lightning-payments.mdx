Otieno sells chapati near a matatu stage. A customer wants to pay 50 shillings in sats. Waiting ten minutes for a block, and paying a <Term id="fee" /> larger than the chapati, makes no sense. This is what the <Term id="lightning" /> is for.

## On-chain or Lightning?

An on-chain payment is written straight into the blockchain. It gets its first <Term id="confirmation" /> when it enters a block, about every 10 minutes, and its fee depends on how busy the network is. It suits large or long-term amounts.

Lightning is a network built on top of Bitcoin. Payments travel through <Term id="channel">channels</Term> between Lightning nodes and arrive in seconds, usually for a fee of a few sats. It suits small, everyday payments.

## Invoices

To receive on Lightning, your wallet makes an <Term id="invoice" />: a payment request, usually shown as a <Term id="qr-code" />. The text form is long and starts with `lnbc`. An invoice contains:

- **the amount** in sats,
- **a description**, such as "2 chapati, Otieno's",
- **an expiry time**, often about an hour, after which it can't be paid.

Each invoice is meant to be paid once. Many wallets also offer a **Lightning address** that looks like an email, such as `otieno@example.com`, which can receive many payments.

## Receiving and sending

**To receive:** open your wallet, tap Receive, enter the amount, and show the QR code or share the invoice.

**To send:** tap Send or Scan, scan the QR code, then **read the amount and description before you confirm**.

<Callout type="scam" title="Lightning payments are final">
There is no reversing a Lightning payment. Check the amount every time. A scammer can stick their own QR code over a shop's poster, so if the description or name looks wrong, ask before paying.
</Callout>

## Try it

Next time you see any payment QR code, at a shop, on a poster or in a message, stop and say out loud: "Who am I paying, how much, and for what?" If you already use a Lightning wallet, ask a friend for an invoice of 10 sats and find the amount, description and expiry before you pay it.
